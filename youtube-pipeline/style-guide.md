# Style Guide — Brand Voice & Visual Identity

> **Every generation step reads this file.** The script generator, metadata generator,
> and any future asset step must load this document verbatim into their prompt so the
> voice does not drift between runs. If you edit this file, the change applies to the
> next generated video — nothing is hard-coded elsewhere.

## Channel identity

- **Niche:** history / micro-history — small, concrete stories that open onto big ideas.
- **Formats:** narrated long-form explainers (8–12 min) + vertical Shorts cut from each video.
- **Promise to the viewer:** "You'll leave knowing one specific true thing you didn't
  know, and why it mattered."

## Tone

- Curious, precise, warm. A knowledgeable friend telling you something genuinely
  interesting — never a lecturer, never a carnival barker.
- Confident about facts, honest about uncertainty. When historians disagree, say so.
  "The records don't tell us" is a feature of the voice, not a weakness.
- Dry humor is welcome; sarcasm at the expense of historical people is not.
- Present tense for narrative beats ("It's 1848. The telegraph line stops at the
  Missouri River."), past tense for analysis.

## Vocabulary

- Concrete nouns over abstractions: "a lead-lined crate of daguerreotypes," not
  "important cultural artifacts."
- Numbers stated plainly and given scale: "40 tons — about eight elephants."
- Banned words/phrases: *shocking, you won't believe, hidden truth THEY don't want,
  insane, mind-blowing, the untold story, dark secret* (unless literally about a
  secret), *delve, tapestry, testament to.*
- Define any term a smart 14-year-old wouldn't know, in half a sentence, in flow.

## Hook formulas (first 15–20 seconds)

Rotate among these; never use the same formula twice in a row:

1. **Object-first:** open on a specific physical thing and its odd detail.
   "This button killed more of Napoleon's soldiers than the Russian army did. Maybe."
2. **Frozen moment:** drop the viewer into one timestamped scene mid-action.
3. **Wrong assumption:** state what everyone believes, then the record that breaks it.
4. **Small question, big answer:** an innocent question whose answer rewires the topic.
   "Why do clocks run clockwise?"
5. **Consequence-first:** start with the ripple effect, rewind to the cause.

A hook must contain **one verifiable specific** (a date, name, number, or place).
No hook may promise more than the video delivers.

## Pacing

- **Long-form:** hook ≤20s → context ≤90s → 3–5 narrative beats of 60–120s each →
  payoff/reframe → single quiet outro line (no "smash that like button" walls; one
  understated ask max, e.g. "If you want more of these, subscribing helps.").
- **Shorts:** one beat only. Hook in the first 2 seconds, resolution by 45s, hard cut.
  No outro. On-screen text mirrors the key nouns and numbers.
- Sentence rhythm: mostly short. One long sentence per paragraph, max. Read every
  script aloud; if a sentence needs a second breath, split it.

## Visual identity

- B-roll: archival images, maps, period photographs, and neutral stock (Pexels).
  Ken Burns motion on stills; no cheesy zoom-punch transitions.
- Text on screen: dates, names, numbers only — never full sentences in long-form.
- Palette: parchment/ink neutrals with a single accent color per video.
- Every synthetic or AI-generated visual/voice element is disclosed via YouTube's
  altered-content flag (the pipeline sets this automatically).

## What we never do

- Never state a legend, rumor, or contested claim as settled fact — label it
  ("the story goes…", "one account claims…") and say what the records support.
- Never invent quotes or dialogue. Paraphrase with attribution instead.
- Never use rage-bait framing, fake cliffhangers, or thumbnails that misrepresent
  the content.
- Never recycle another channel's framing, structure, or thumbnail concept.
- Never publish a script whose factual claims haven't been checked against the
  originality checklist in review.
